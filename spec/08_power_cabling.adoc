== Power and Interconnect Cabling: Belden 8267

* *Mains AC power cable*: Belden 8267 (RG-213/U flexible coax, 50 Ω, 13 AWG, PVC jacket)
* *3 × 2 m runs*:
. Run 1: Enclosure AC inlet → CCPF-1700 AC input (L, N, Earth); shield to enclosure frame
. Run 2: CCPF AC ground → rig chassis ground (star-point safety earth)
. Run 3: E-stop circuit loop (redundant mains disconnect path)
* Shield grounded *single-point* at enclosure frame only (avoid ground loops)
* Signal cabling: STP with ferrite clamps for V_PROG/V_MON/INHIBIT/EOC; twisted pair for gate drive
* Discharge loop: *wide flat copper busbars only* (no cable); short heavy copper tubing or braided strap from SCR to reactor

=== Discharge Flex Link Alternatives

The default discharge architecture per the loop note above uses braided copper strap or heavy copper tubing from SCR to reactor. The notes below cover two adjacent topics: operational care if a flexible coax (RG-213 / Belden 8267) is substituted for the strap, and upgrade paths for higher cycle life or longer-run installations.

==== Option: RG-213 (Belden 8267) as discharge flex link

Acceptable for short flex runs when mechanical compliance to a removable reactor assembly is needed. Not a long-term low-maintenance choice; needs scheduled inspection.

* *Length*: ≤ 1 m (0.5 m preferred). Resistive loss is ~1.8% of shot energy per meter at design conditions; per-meter ΔT is ~17 K per shot. Going beyond ~1 m stresses the PVC jacket (rated 80 °C) under repeated shots.
* *Terminations*: crimped lugs only, no solder (solder joints fatigue under repeated pulse heating). Bond braid circumferentially to the return path at both ends; do not pigtail the braid to a single point.
* *Replacement schedule*: every 10,000–20,000 shots, or sooner if inspection signals trigger. Sync with capacitor service intervals where possible.
* *Inspection protocol*:
** Visual check of terminations every ~500 shots — discoloration, jacket damage near lugs
** IR thermometer reading of jacket ~30 s after a shot — track trend, not absolute
** End-to-end DC resistance with milliohmmeter every cap swap — log baseline; 50% increase = replace
** Immediate replacement on: audible snap during discharge, visible jacket damage, resistance > 2× baseline

==== Upgrade paths

Listed in order of incremental improvement over RG-213. The bigger conductor cross-sections and proper pulse-rated constructions remove the inspection-heavy maintenance pattern.

[cols="2,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Cable | Conductor area | V rating | Cost (5 m pair) | Expected life

| RG-213 (Belden 8267)        | 5.3 mm²  | 3.7 kV | ~$30   | 10–20k shots
| RG-218/U                     | ~21 mm²  | 5 kV   | ~$150  | 30–50k shots
| Type W 2/0 portable power    | 67 mm²   | 2 kV   | ~$200  | 100k+ shots
| Spot welder kickless cable   | 107+ mm² | 2–5 kV | ~$500+ | 1M+ shots
|===

* *RG-218/U*: incremental upgrade — same coaxial geometry, scaled-up conductor and dielectric.
* *Type W 2/0 portable power*: workhorse upgrade — heavily stranded portable power cable, EPDM jacket, industrial robust. Run two conductors twisted or zip-tied at ~100 mm intervals to keep the inductive loop area small. Terminate with hydraulic-crimped compression lugs.
* *Spot welder kickless cable*: industrial-pedigree option — coaxial geometry built in, designed for millions of pulse cycles. Suppliers: Watteredge, NWL Capacitors, Mersen, Hi-Tech Industries. Water-cooled variants available for sustained high-rate operation.
* For sustained operation > 10 shots/min or runs > 2 m, Type W or kickless cable is recommended over scaling RG-213.
