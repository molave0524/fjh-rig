== Reaction Chamber

=== Tube (baseline) — LOCKED

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Spec | Value

| Dimensions    | *18 mm ID × 21.6 mm OD, 1.8 mm wall*
| Material      | Clear fused quartz
| Source        | *Wale Apparatus CFQ18X21.6* (~$55/tube, 4 ft length)
| Cut length    | ~100–150 mm per shot column
| Realistic life | 200–500 shots at 30 s cycles with active cooling
|===

.Rationale for 18 mm ID over 20 mm ID
For 1 g feedstock at 14 kJ delivered, 18 mm geometry concentrates energy more effectively:

* *18 mm cross-section*: ~254 mm² → *~55 J/mm² energy flux*
* *20 mm cross-section*: ~314 mm² → ~45 J/mm² energy flux

Higher current density matches published FJH process parameters (Tour group: 15–20 mm reactors for 1 g samples). Future scaling to 2+ g requires electrode redesign anyway; 18 mm baseline does not preclude that upgrade.

=== Tube (upgrade path) — Hot-pressed BN

* Hot-pressed boron nitride (HBT BN), 18 mm ID, $200/tube
* Tolerates aggressive forced cooling without thermal-shock failure modes
* *Softer than quartz* (Mohs ~2 vs ~7) — use sacrificial graphite or hard-ceramic spacer between metal electrode and BN end
* *Higher CTE* (4 × 10⁻⁶ /K vs 0.5 × 10⁻⁶ /K for quartz) — leave clearance in any tight metal collar
* Published FJH data on BN tubes is thin; the 1000+ shot figure is extrapolated from broader pulsed-power and ceramic-fatigue literature

=== Electrodes — LOCKED: Architecture 4 (graphite puck + Cu push rod)

For the 18 mm ID quartz tube and 1 g carbon-black feedstock, the electrode architecture is *Architecture 4: graphite puck + small-bore copper push rod*. The contact face inside the tube is full-bore graphite-on-carbon-on-graphite. Current is carried in by smaller-diameter copper push rods that act on the outer faces of the pucks; the push rod is fully decoupled from the electrical contact face and from the tube ID.

.Architectures considered
[cols="1,3,2", options="header"]
|===
| Arch | Configuration | Why not (vs. selected)

| 1 | Solid Cu rod, full-bore           | Cu inclusions in product; face erosion; pyrometry contamination from Cu I lines (510/515/521 nm)
| 2 | Solid graphite rod, full-bore      | 17.5 mm OD seal interface harder than necessary; brittle full-length rod is a handling liability
| 3 | Cu rod with replaceable graphite end cap | Acceptable purity, but full 17.5 mm OD working face must translate inside the tube — much harder seal problem than the 10 mm push rod
| *4* | *Graphite puck + Cu push rod (selected)* | Cleanest electrical interface (carbon-on-carbon-on-carbon); simplest seal (10 mm smooth rod); simplest reload (drop-in puck); pucks batch-producible; pyrometry stays clean
|===

[cols="2,3", options="header"]
|===
| Component | Spec

| *Graphite puck* (sacrificial contact face) | Isotropic high-density graphite (POCO EDM-3, Mersen 2333, or equivalent). *17.5 mm OD* (tube ID − 0.5 mm clearance). *8–10 mm thick* baseline (5–15 mm acceptable). Both faces flat, perpendicular to axis, ~32 µin finish. *2 per shot*; held by column compression; discarded with column residue between shots.
| *Copper push rod* (current carrier) | C110 OFHC. *10 mm OD* × 150–250 mm length (set by gland geometry and compression train). Ground to ≤16 µin over the sealing length. End-treatment TBD (see Q3 in pending). 2 per shot.
|===

.Why Arch 4 over Arch 3 (the more common published configuration)
* Compressed sample column is only 5.6–8.4 mm tall (per CB height table). With Arch 3, the full 17.5 mm electrode face must translate inside the tube — forces the sliding seal to ride on a 17.5 mm OD surface vs. a 10 mm push rod
* Carbon-on-carbon-on-carbon contact is the cleanest possible electrical interface; most stable contact resistance shot-to-shot
* Pucks are batch-producible: ~30 pucks from a single 18 mm × 300 mm graphite rod (~$60 of stock)
* No Cu I lines (510.5 / 515.3 / 521.8 nm) to mask out of pyrometry Planck fits

.Cross-section margin
10 mm Cu rod = 78.5 mm² — wildly over-spec'd for the 4200 A peak pulse. Resistive heating in the 250 mm push rod is on the order of mΩ, negligible against the 0.3–1 Ω column.

=== Electrode operating envelope

* Compression set by screw or pneumatic actuator with load-cell feedback
* *Target sample bulk resistance: 1–10 Ω*
** Below ~0.5 Ω: discharge too fast, ringing dominates
** Above ~20 Ω: rectifier-side losses dominate before sample heats fully

=== Open electrode design questions

Six open items (Q1–Q6) remain in the electrode subsystem, tracked as `ELECTRODE-Q1` through `ELECTRODE-Q6` in `data/pending.yaml`:

* *Q1*: Puck stock and grade (POCO EDM-3 / Mersen 2333 / surplus EDM)
* *Q2*: Puck-to-rod contact face — flat-on-flat or counterbored capture
* *Q3*: Push-rod end treatment — bare Cu, bonded graphite disc, or threaded graphite tip
* *Q4*: Sliding seal / gland — PTFE bushing, graphite-impregnated, or O-ring + wiper
* *Q5*: Compression train interface (load cell, lever, pneumatic ram, hand screw)
* *Q6*: Tube-end termination — depends on Q4 (gland design)

NOTE: Q2 is the recommended next decision because it ripples backward into puck specification. Once fixed, the 30-puck batch can be cut and Q3–Q6 proceed in parallel.

=== Carbon black sample column heights (18 mm ID, 2.54 cm² cross-section)

Furnace blacks run 0.10–0.25 g/cm³ loose (sometimes as low as 0.05 g/cm³ for extra-fluffy grades). Under FJH compression (5–50 MPa axial), they pack to roughly 0.7 g/cm³.

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| Mass | Loose (~0.2 g/cm³) | Compressed (~0.7 g/cm³, resting state)

| 1.0 g  | ~20 mm | ~5.6 mm
| 1.25 g | ~25 mm | ~7.0 mm
| 1.5 g  | ~30 mm | ~8.4 mm
|===

IMPORTANT: Resting-state heights (5.6–8.4 mm) define electrode spacing tolerance. Compression mechanism must span 6–10 mm with *<0.2 mm runout*. Load-cell feedback essential.
