== Executive Summary

**Build-readiness assessment: Spec is well-structured but not yet build-ready.**

A skilled builder handed the current document set could not construct the rig without substantial additional design and decision input. The capacitor bank, charging supply, discharge switch fixture, and reactor tube subsystems are at near-buildable detail. The electrode column, sample handling, enclosure/chassis, atmosphere plumbing, and control electronics are at conceptual or BOM-only detail. Three of four output folders (`schematics/`, `mechanical/`, `firmware/`) are empty placeholders.

**Top blockers (must resolve before fabrication can begin):**

1. **No schematics exist.** `schematics/` folder is empty. Control electronics cannot be built from block diagrams alone.
2. **No mechanical drawings exist.** `mechanical/` folder is empty. Even fully-specified subsystems (e.g., the SCR fixture) need fab drawings.
3. **No firmware exists.** `firmware/` folder is empty.
4. **Sample handling completely undesigned** (`DESIGN-SAMPLE-HANDLING`). Biggest remaining mechanical task.
5. **Electrode column: 6 of 6 design questions open** (`ELECTRODE-Q1` through `Q6`). Architecture 4 is locked but the actual implementation is not.

**Top consistency issues:**

1. **Cycle-time architecture is underdocumented** (Section 10) — the 25–30 s cycle works only if a parallel prep station pre-prepares the next tube during charging. Spec lists "sample handling 20–25 s" without indicating this is parallel prep time, not inline tube-swap time. Inline swap is ~5 s. The prep station itself is undocumented.
2. **Bank ESR figure was wrong** (now corrected in this round) — actual per-cap ESR is 13 mΩ per Kemet datasheet, not the ~10 mΩ implied in earlier spec nor the 24 mΩ I quoted earlier in audit. Bank ESR (14 cap parallel) = 0.93 mΩ.
3. **Belden 8267 for mains AC is physically problematic** — RG-213/U is 2-conductor (center + shield), but mains AC requires L/N/Earth (3 conductors).
4. **Bus bar geometry described two different ways** in Section 02 vs Section 08.

**Where this rig deviates from published FJH practice:**

* Electrode Architecture 4 (separate puck + push rod) is novel; Tour 2020 patent and most published implementations use Arch 3 (Cu rod with bonded graphite end cap). The choice is well-reasoned but unverified.
* Bus bar length (~80 cm) is long for low-inductance discharge loop; published systems tend toward <30 cm rails.
* Higher per-cap energy (1.44 kJ/cap) than Tour 2020 reference (0.88 kJ/cap), indicating per-cap stress is *higher*, not lower as Section 02 implies.

**Recommended order of work before build:**

. Resolve cycle-time/architecture inconsistencies (1–2 hours of doc work)
. Resolve `ELECTRODE-Q2` (puck contact geometry) — unblocks Q3 and puck batch
. Design tube-end termination (`ELECTRODE-Q4`/`Q6`) — unblocks atmosphere integration
. Produce SCR fixture mechanical drawings (1 day CAD)
. Produce gate driver + ESP32 carrier schematics (2–3 days)
. Complete sample-handling design (1–2 weeks)
. Develop commissioning SOP from skeleton (1–2 days)

=== C6. Cycle-time architecture not documented (parallel prep station)

**Location:** `spec/10_fsm_timing.adoc`, `spec/00_overview.adoc`

**Description:** The cycle math is consistent at ~27 s *if* the operator pre-prepares the next sample tube at a separate prep station while the bank charges. Confirmed operational model:

* Charge branch: 20 s (charge) + 1 s (FIRE) + 0.75 s (dump-bleed) ≈ 22 s
* Inline sample handling: **~5 s** (tube swap only — next tube already loaded, compressed, and verified at the prep station)
* Total inline cycle: ~27 s ✓ matches 25–30 s claim

The math works. But the spec doesn't document this. Concrete documentation gaps:

. **The "20–25 s sample handling" figure in Section 10 is misleading.** It represents parallel prep time at a separate station, not inline cycle time. A builder reading the spec cold sees "20 s charge + 20–25 s sample handling" and reasonably interprets these as serial → reaches 45 s, contradicting the 25–30 s headline.

. **The prep station itself is undocumented.** The architecture diagram in Section 00 shows only the reaction chamber, not the prep station. Open questions:
** Where does prep happen? Inside the enclosure? At an adjacent benchtop?
** What's the prep workflow? (Weigh, load into spare tube, compress, verify R, set aside?)
** What handoff mechanism transfers the prepped tube from prep station into the reaction position? Manual swap? Sliding stage? Cassette?
** How many tubes / electrode sets are in rotation? (Two — one in-station, one in-prep — is the minimum.)
** What's the prep station equipment list? (Scale, compression jig, R-meter, parking station for next tube.)

. **Two-FSM architecture in firmware** (`firmware/README.adoc`) makes sense in this context (one FSM for the rig, one for the prep station / operator) but isn't connected to the spec narrative.

. **Operator safety during parallel prep** is undefined:
** Is the operator inside or outside the enclosure during charging? (Must be outside for HV safety.)
** Door interlock locks during `CHARGE` state, but operator needs to access the prep station — is the prep station inside the same enclosure (interlock-protected from the bank) or in a separate physical space?
** What signals the operator that the rig is ready for tube transfer? (Visual indicator? Audio?)

**Recommended action:** Add a "Sample Prep Workflow" subsection (probably in `spec/04_reaction_chamber.adoc` adjacent to the existing electrode and column sections, or a new `spec/04b_sample_prep.adoc`). Cover:

* Prep station physical layout
* Prep workflow (weigh → load → compress → R-verify → park)
* Handoff mechanism for tube transfer
* Operator safety during parallel ops
* Inventory of consumables in rotation (tubes, pucks, push rods)

Then update Section 10 cycle timing to distinguish inline (~5 s) from parallel (~20–25 s) handling time. Update Section 00 architecture diagram to include the prep station.

This is a substantive documentation gap because it touches the rig's operational model — without it, the cycle-time claim is not reproducible by a third party. The `DESIGN-SAMPLE-HANDLING` pending item partially overlaps but is framed as a mechanization question; this is also a workflow / station / documentation question.

**Effort:** MEDIUM (workflow doc + station design) — partially overlaps with `DESIGN-SAMPLE-HANDLING` (C4 above)

== Findings — High Severity

These findings affect successful operation, safety margins, or major engineering questions.

=== H1. Inrush resistor 500 W rating likely inadequate

**Location:** `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`, `data/bom.csv` line 5

**Description:** Spec calls for ~25 Ω / 500 W inrush resistor with τ ≈ 6.3 s. Analysis:

* Energy dissipated in inrush resistor per charge cycle = 0.5 × CV² (lost in resistor during charging) ≈ 10 kJ (the other 10 kJ stored in caps)
* At 30 s cycle, average dissipation = 10000/30 ≈ **333 W** — within 500 W rating but only 33% margin
* At faster cycles (e.g., 10 s) average dissipation = 1000 W — well over rating
* Peak instantaneous power at start of charge = V²/R = 160000/25 = **6.4 kW**, lasting until inrush current decays — a 500 W resistor will see severe thermal transient

Standard practice: derate by 2× for transient handling. A 1 kW or 1.5 kW non-inductive power resistor would be more appropriate.

The `SIZE-INRUSH` pending item exists but no analysis has been done.

**Recommended action:** Compute the actual i²t profile under cold-bank charge, including thermal time constant of the resistor. Likely upsize to 1 kW. Add the analysis to spec.

**Effort:** LOW (analysis); MEDIUM (re-source)

=== H2. Bus bar length is long for low-inductance discharge

**Location:** `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`

**Description:** Spec calls for 25 mm × 6 mm copper rails, ~80 cm length each. For comparison:

* Tour 2020 patent and most published bench-scale FJH systems use bus bars <30 cm
* Loop inductance scales with rail length and rail spacing
* For 25 × 6 mm rails at typical 25–50 mm spacing, inductance is roughly 0.3–0.6 µH/m → total bank-loop inductance ~0.25–0.5 µH for 80 cm rails
* This is comparable to or larger than the load inductance and will slow rise time and reduce peak current

`VERIFY-BUSBAR-L` is tracked but no predicted value or measurement plan exists.

**Recommended action:** Either (a) reduce bus bar length to ≤40 cm by reorganizing the 7×2 grid to 5×3 or 4×4 with center SCR mount; or (b) accept the inductance and document the predicted impact on peak current.

**Effort:** MEDIUM (chassis re-layout) or LOW (accept + document)

=== H4. Belden 8267 for mains AC is physically problematic

**Location:** `spec/08_power_cabling.adoc`, `data/bom.csv` line 42

**Description:** Belden 8267 / RG-213/U is a **2-conductor coaxial cable** (center conductor + shield). Mains AC requires **3 conductors** (Line + Neutral + Earth) for safe installation per NEC and most electrical codes.

The spec states Run 1 carries "L, N, Earth" through Belden 8267 — this is not physically possible in a single coax run. Options:

. Use proper 3-conductor power cable (e.g., 14/3 or 12/3 SOOW) for Run 1 → not Belden 8267
. Use one coax for L+shield-as-return and a separate conductor for Earth → unconventional and likely code violation
. Use the coax only for HF interfaces (not AC mains) and source different cable for power

The recent Section 08 addition discusses Belden 8267 as a *discharge flex link alternative* (RF coax for kA pulse) but does not address the mains-AC misuse.

**Recommended action:** Replace Belden 8267 for AC mains with proper 3-conductor power cable. Keep Belden 8267 (if desired) only for non-power roles. Re-write Section 08 to reflect actual cabling.

**Effort:** LOW (re-source + doc rewrite)

=== H5. Bank ESR figure was wrong (now corrected)

**Location:** `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`, "Per-cap loading" block

**Description:** Spec previously stated: "Aggregate bank ESR drops from ~1.0 mΩ → ~0.71 mΩ (estimated, ~14 caps in parallel)."

The correct per-cap ESR from the Kemet ALS70A183QS400 datasheet (confirmed via Digikey listing) is **13 mΩ at 100 Hz, 20 °C**. The numbers in the spec used an unsourced ~10 mΩ per-cap figure.

Correct values in pure parallel:
* 10 caps: 13 / 10 = **1.30 mΩ**
* 14 caps: 13 / 14 = **0.93 mΩ**

So actual bank ESR is **~30% higher** than the spec stated, not the "~2× higher" my audit originally claimed (which was based on a wrong 24 mΩ per-cap figure I had quoted from memory earlier in the project — also wrong).

**Status: RESOLVED.** Spec corrected; appendix confabulation history updated to log both the earlier ~10 mΩ assumption and my own ~24 mΩ misquote.

**Downstream impact (improves margins):**

With the corrected 0.93 mΩ bank ESR:

* Bank-loss fraction at 1 Ω sample load: ~0.09% (vs ~0.2% in prior audit math)
* Per-shot energy in bank: ~13 J total, ~0.93 J per cap
* Per-cap power at 30 s cycle: 0.031 W
* Core temperature rise: ~0.4 K above ambient

The bank has **even more thermal headroom than previously calculated**. The 30 s cycle remains well within the no-aging-acceleration regime. Cap pulse-life conclusions (20–50k shots realistic, 30 s cycle is in the flat regime of the Arrhenius curve) stand unchanged.

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

NOTE: This finding is left in the audit for historical traceability. ESR-derived figures elsewhere in the spec (e.g., predicted discharge waveform, M6 below) should be re-derived from the corrected 0.93 mΩ baseline when added.

=== H6. ALS70A183QS400 pulse-life caveat missing from spec — RESOLVED

**Location:** `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`

**Description:** The ALS70 series is a UPS/inverter electrolytic, not pulse-rated. Realistic pulse life under FJH duty is 20,000–50,000 shots; the datasheet's 20,000-hour endurance figure is an Arrhenius-governed time-dependent metric for ripple service and does not characterize shot-count life. Failure modes (ESR climb, capacitance drop, sudden ESR spike, vent operation) and monitoring protocol were not captured anywhere in the spec.

**Resolution:** Added new `=== Pulse Life and Service Expectations` subsection to `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc` covering:

* **Mechanism analysis**: Two independent aging paths (per-time Arrhenius vs per-shot electrochemical/mechanical), with this rig deep in the no-aging-acceleration thermal regime (~0.4 K core rise at 30 s cycle), so shot-count is the binding constraint.
* **Realistic pulse life table**: 20–50k shots for ALS70, 100k–1M for pulse-rated Al electrolytic (Vishay 159 PUL-SI, CDE 947D), 1M+ for film caps.
* **Failure-mode indicators**: ESR climb, capacitance drop, sudden ESR spike, vent operation.
* **Monitoring protocol**: per-cap ESR baseline at installation, periodic re-measurement every ~5,000 shots, replacement thresholds (2× baseline for individual cap, 1.5× average for full bank).
* **Upgrade path**: pulse-rated capacitor families with explicit note that upgrade is *not* required for first-build operation.

Also added pending item `VERIFY-CAP-ESR-BASELINE` for the installation measurement; tightened `FUTURE-FILM-CAPS` with specific candidates (Vishay 159 PUL-SI / CDE 947D).

**Status: RESOLVED.**

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

=== H11. R_sample target range wider than published optimum — RESOLVED

**Location:** `spec/04_reaction_chamber.adoc`, "Electrode operating envelope"

**Description:** Spec target is 1–10 Ω sample bulk resistance. Published Tour-group work and the FJH literature reviewed (Tan 2026; MDPI 2024 pencil graphite study) cluster around 0.5–2 Ω for optimum yield with carbon-black feedstocks.

**Resolution:** Confirmed by project owner that the wider 1–10 Ω range is intentional. Rationale: this rig's intended feedstock variety (carbon black, calcined coke, biochar) yields a range of achievable compressed-state resistances depending on grade and packing density. Higher R_sample (3–10 Ω) produces functional flash-graphene product, though Raman 2D peak quality and turbostratic stacking may be modestly lower than at the Tour optimum.

The 0.5 Ω lower bound and 20 Ω upper bound (already in spec) are the *hard* failure modes. Within those bounds, the operator tunes per-feedstock.

Spec updated with a literature-comparison note explaining the rationale, so future readers don't interpret the wider range as a documentation error against published values.

**Status: RESOLVED.**

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

=== H12. HV divider ratio underspecified — RESOLVED

**Location:** `spec/09_diagnostics.adoc`, `data/bom.csv` line 44

**Description:** Spec originally called for "10:1 or 100:1, BNC out" HV divider. Choice matters:

* 10:1: 400 V → 40 V at scope input. Most scope BNC inputs accept ≤300 V CAT I but the 40 V peak risks transient excursion beyond limits during discharge ringing.
* 100:1: 400 V → 4 V at scope input. Within scope tolerance, but resolution is reduced.

**Resolution:** Locked at **100:1** in spec and BOM. Rationale captured inline: scope-side excursion stays below 4 V even under transient ringing, well within standard 1× probe ratings. Modest resolution loss is acceptable for the 0–400 V operating range; the scope's vertical sensitivity at 1 V/div easily resolves the discharge waveform shape.

Spec also locks **≥10 MHz bandwidth** to capture discharge-edge transients.

**Status: RESOLVED.**

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

=== H13. Per-cap fuse rating unspecified — RESOLVED

**Location:** `data/bom.csv` line 9; `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`

**Description:** Spec originally called for "fast-blow rated for cap fault" but no current rating, voltage rating, or interrupt rating. The non-obvious issue: ordinary fast-blow fuses (Bussmann KTK, Mersen ATQR, Littelfuse KLK) have i²t pre-arc ratings in the single-digit A²·s range — they would clear on the first FJH shot at 300 A per-cap pulse current.

What's actually needed is a **capacitor-bank protection fuse**, a specialty semiconductor-protection class with high i²t pre-arc rating to survive repeated discharge pulses while still clearing in single-cap-short fault conditions.

**Resolution:** Locked at **Mersen A50QS80-4** (80 A continuous, 500 V DC, semiconductor-protection class) in spec and BOM. Sized for the estimated ~4,500 A²·s per-shot pulse i²t with ~30% margin against the fuse pre-arc rating.

Spec includes explicit warning *not* to substitute ordinary fast-blow fuses. Added pending item `VERIFY-FUSE-ITT` for first-commissioning measurement of actual per-cap pulse i²t — can downsize to A50QS50-4 if margin proves generous.

**Status: RESOLVED.**

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

=== H14. Freewheel diode specifics absent — RESOLVED

**Location:** `data/bom.csv` line 8; `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc`

**Description:** Single BOM line: "High-current diode across bank, 1, $15-30, various, sourcing, Reverse-polarity protection." Missing polarity, current/voltage ratings, and specific part candidate.

**Resolution:** Locked at **Vishay VS-VSKT160-12** (160 A average forward, 1200 V reverse, stud-mount standard rectifier) in spec and BOM.

Spec notes mounting (M16 stud + small finned heatsink near negative bus bar) and polarity (cathode to bank+).

**Status: RESOLVED.**

**Effort:** RESOLVED.

=== M6. No predicted discharge waveform

**Location:** Nowhere in spec

**Description:** No predicted peak current, rise time, ringing frequency, or discharge envelope is documented. Without prediction, the first hot shot's diagnostic measurements have no reference to compare against — verification becomes "did anything obviously break" rather than "did the waveform match expectation."

For the discharge loop (C = 0.252 F, R_load ≈ 1 Ω, L_loop ≈ 0.3–0.5 µH estimated):

* Damping ratio: ζ = R/(2√(L/C)) ≈ 1 × √(0.252/0.5e-6) ≈ 700 → heavily overdamped
* Time constant: τ ≈ RC ≈ 0.25 s
* Peak current: ~V/R ≈ 400 A (load-limited, not inductance-limited)

These rough numbers suggest the discharge is overdamped and load-resistance-limited, **not** ringing/inductance-limited as commonly assumed for FJH discharges.

**Recommended action:** Add a predicted-waveform subsection to `spec/02_capacitor_bank.adoc` or `spec/09_diagnostics.adoc` with expected peak current, half-pulse duration, and waveshape under nominal load.

**Effort:** LOW (analysis); LOW (doc)

=== M14. Dump SCR / contactor pulse rating concern

**Location:** `data/bom.csv` line 7

**Description:** "Dump SCR/contactor — LEV200 or equivalent — $60–130, Tyco/various." The LEV200 is a 200 A continuous DC contactor (Tyco Kilovac series). It is *not* designed for kA-level pulse switching.

The dump-bleed path is documented as ~750 ms, presumably through the bleed resistor (12.5 kΩ). At 400 V across 12.5 kΩ + LEV200 contact resistance, the dump current is ~32 mA — well within LEV200 ratings.

But for *emergency* dump (e.g., E-stop with bank near 400 V), the LEV200 must make-and-hold while the bank discharges through the bleed. The initial in-rush current is V/R_loop, dominated by the bleed resistor (~32 mA peak). LEV200 handles this fine.

**However:** If the operator concept is that the dump-SCR completes a *short-circuit* across the bank (bypassing the bleed), the LEV200 would see 400 V across negligible resistance → kA inrush → exceeds LEV200's break rating immediately.

The spec is ambiguous about which architecture is intended. The dump-bleed path through the bleed resistor is OK; a true crowbar dump is not.

**Recommended action:** Clarify dump architecture explicitly: is dump = "close LEV200 to engage bleed path" or "close LEV200 to crowbar bank"? Spec the right component for the chosen architecture.

**Effort:** LOW

== Closing Notes

The spec is at a good level of detail for the subsystems that have been worked through (cap bank, SCR, tube, charging supply), but coverage is uneven. The strongest sections (cap bank, SCR fixture after the recent updates, vacuum gauge selection) are nearly buildable. The largest single risk is that **the rig as currently documented cannot be handed to a builder for parallel execution.** Several subsystems block each other (electrode Q4 blocks Q6 blocks tube termination blocks atmosphere integration), so resolution must be largely sequential. The 4–8 week design timeline above assumes one engineer working full-time.

A realistic path forward: **resolve the cheap documentation fixes this week** (most can be done in an evening), then prioritize the electrode `Q2`/`Q4`/`Q6` chain and the sample-handling design as the longest-pole items. The enclosure and EMI work can run in parallel once the internal geometry firms up.

The project is well-positioned for a private build with continued iteration. It is not yet positioned for handing-off as a complete reproducible design package.
